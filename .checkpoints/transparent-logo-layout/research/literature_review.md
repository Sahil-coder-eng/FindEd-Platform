# Literature Review — Education Loan Comparison Platform

## 1. Overview

This literature review examines existing academic research, industry platforms,
and government initiatives related to education financing, loan comparison,
financial literacy, and student decision-support systems.

## 2. Academic Research

### 2.1 Education Financing & Student Debt

**Area:** The growing burden of education loans globally and in India.

- Research has documented the rapid growth in education loan disbursement in India,
  particularly after the introduction of the Model Educational Loan Scheme by IBA
  (Indian Banks' Association).
- Studies highlight that many borrowers do not fully understand the total cost of
  their loans at the time of borrowing, particularly the impact of interest
  capitalization during moratorium periods.
- **Research gap:** Limited tools exist that transparently show the full financial
  impact of moratorium-period interest capitalization on total borrowing cost.

### 2.2 Financial Literacy Among Students

**Area:** Student understanding of loan terms and financial concepts.

- Multiple studies (across India, US, UK) consistently find that students and parents
  have limited understanding of compound interest, amortization, and the distinction
  between interest rates and total borrowing cost.
- Financial literacy interventions that use interactive calculators and visual tools
  have shown measurable improvements in understanding.
- **Research gap:** Most existing education-loan calculators provide only EMI output
  without explaining the underlying mathematics or showing the full repayment journey.

### 2.3 Decision Support Systems in Finance

**Area:** Use of technology to support financial decision-making.

- Decision support systems (DSS) in personal finance have been studied extensively.
  Effective systems share common traits: transparency, comparison capability,
  scenario analysis, and clear presentation of tradeoffs.
- **Research gap:** Few decision-support systems specifically target education-loan
  comparison with integrated moratorium analysis, scenario simulation, and source
  transparency.

## 3. Existing Platforms & Tools

### 3.1 Bank EMI Calculators

Most Indian banks (SBI, HDFC, Axis, etc.) provide basic EMI calculators on their
websites.

**Typical capabilities:**
- Single-lender EMI calculation
- Basic principal/rate/tenure input

**Limitations:**
- No multi-lender comparison
- No moratorium impact analysis
- No repayment schedule detail
- No scenario or sensitivity analysis
- No source transparency or data dating

### 3.2 Loan Aggregator Platforms

Platforms such as BankBazaar, PaisaBazaar, and similar services aggregate loan
offers from multiple lenders.

**Typical capabilities:**
- Multi-lender listing
- Rate comparison
- Some provide EMI calculation

**Limitations:**
- Often lead-generation focused (collect user data for lender referrals)
- Limited or no transparent amortization schedule
- Moratorium analysis typically absent
- Scenario/sensitivity analysis not available
- Source and verification transparency varies

### 3.3 Government Portals

The Vidya Lakshmi Portal (Government of India) connects students with
education-loan schemes.

**Typical capabilities:**
- Scheme listing from participating banks
- Application submission

**Limitations:**
- Not a comparison/analysis tool
- No EMI calculation or scenario analysis
- No repayment visualization

### 3.4 International Platforms

Platforms like Prodigy Finance, MPOWER Financing, and others serve international
students.

**Typical capabilities:**
- Loan offerings for select universities
- Online application

**Limitations:**
- Lender-specific (not comparison platforms)
- Limited analytical tools

## 4. Research Gaps Identified

Based on this literature review, the following gaps emerge:

| # | Gap | Evidence |
|---|-----|----------|
| 1 | No integrated platform combining EMI, amortization, moratorium analysis, and multi-lender comparison | Bank calculators are single-lender; aggregators focus on lead generation |
| 2 | Moratorium impact (especially interest capitalization) is poorly communicated | Academic research confirms student confusion about compound interest effects |
| 3 | Scenario and sensitivity analysis absent from student-facing tools | Existing calculators provide single-point outputs, not "what-if" analysis |
| 4 | Source transparency lacking | Most platforms do not clearly date or source their rate information |
| 5 | Total cost (beyond EMI) rarely computed transparently | Processing fees, charges, and moratorium costs are often omitted or unclear |
| 6 | International education cost planning not integrated with loan comparison | Currency conversion and exchange-rate impact are handled separately |

## 5. Our Contribution

This project proposes an **integrated and transparent framework** that addresses
these gaps by combining:

1. Standard EMI calculation with full mathematical explanation
2. Multi-lender comparison across documented, sourced attributes
3. Complete amortization schedule with visualization
4. Moratorium impact simulator (paid-during vs. capitalized)
5. Total cost estimation including applicable charges with transparency
6. Scenario and sensitivity analysis
7. International education cost planning with currency impact
8. Source transparency with verification dates

**This is a proposed contribution, not a claim that no similar system exists.**
The contribution lies in the *integration* of these features in a student-friendly,
transparent, and research-backed interface.
